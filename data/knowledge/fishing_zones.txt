Potential Fishing Zones (PFZ)

PFZ stands for Potential Fishing Zone. Potential Fishing Zones are specific areas in the ocean where fish are likely to aggregate or be found in higher abundance.

How PFZs are Identified:
Potential Fishing Zones are identified by scientists using satellite ocean remote sensing data, including:
1. Sea Surface Temperature (SST) gradients and ocean fronts, where cooler nutrient-rich water meets warmer water.
2. Chlorophyll-a concentration, which indicates phytoplankton and micro-algae growth forming the base of the marine food chain.
3. Ocean color and current convergence zones where small fish, baitfish, and pelagic species congregate.

Advisory Nature and Practical Limitations:
PFZ advisories are recommendations and scientific estimations, not a 100% guarantee of fish availability. Fish are living creatures influenced by depth, local currents, time of day, and lunar cycles. 

Safety and Fishermen Guidelines:
Fishermen should always combine PFZ advisory locations with:
- Current weather, wind speed, and wave height conditions.
- Vessel seaworthiness, engine capacity, and fuel range.
- Official marine warnings from IMD, INCOIS, and Coast Guard.
- Local traditional fishing knowledge.
Never venture into distant PFZs if severe weather or high wave warnings are in effect.
